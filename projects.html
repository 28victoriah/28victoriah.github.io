Independent Research

The Effect of Different Light Colors on the Eating Habits of Plastic-consuming Superworms and Mealworms

Abstract: Plastic is detrimental to the environment, humans, and animals while being incredibly difficult to degrade. It can pollute soil and water, leading to crop failure and contaminated drinking water and food. Microplastics can cause health issues, and they are also ubiquitous. In order to reduce the current research gap on the biodegradation of plastic, my experiment tests the effect of different light colors on the eating habits of plastic-consuming superworms and mealworms while they are provided with expanded polystyrene (EPS) as the only source of food to find out which light color provides the most encouraging environment for superworms and mealworms to consume as much of the EPS as possible. The objective of this experiment is to understand the effect of different light colors on the eating habits of plastic-consuming superworms and mealworms by comparing the amount of plastic eaten before and after the experiment. I tested red light, green light, blue light, and white light with a control group that was exposed to no light. My experiment showed that superworms were more effective at consuming plastic compared to mealworms, and white light should be used as it had the most positive effect on the eating habits of superworms, athough no light is also a great second option.

Impact: The significance of this experiment is finding a more eco-friendly, healthy, and cost effective method compared to the current methods while filling in the research gap. Furthermore, it can be used to maximize plastic biodegradation if this method of using insects becomes valid for industrial practice. So far researchers have neither quantified the amount of plastic eaten by these insect larvae nor have they compared how different light color environments may affect the amount of plastic consumed by these insects. My design of the experiment aligns with my research objective to find a better way to degrade plastic through a much less costly method with insect larvae and eco friendly and healthier to people. Additionally, it will help me and other researchers understand the effects of different light colors on the eating habits of plastic-eating superworms and mealworms and find the most efficient conditions for them, allowing them to eat as much as possible within the time frame.

Engagement: I partnered with the San Francisco Bay National Wildlife Refuge Education Center to present research results at its Earth Day Kick-Off meeting to an audience of 40 people (including over 10 children), successfully raising public awareness about plastic pollution.

ASDRP Research

Optimized pathway for designing small molecules with a hybrid quantum classical generative adversarial network.

Abstract: The modern process of drug discovery and development requires large financial investment and time, which is why researchers are utilizing new computational chemistry methods like machine learning to speed up molecular synthesis pathways. Alzheimer's disease is a neurodegenerative disease associated with the aggregation of many proteins, including butyrylcholinesterase (BChE). The treatment requires molecules that can penetrate the extremely selective blood-brain barrier (BBB) and inhibit BChE. Our model architecture utilizes a Wasserstein generative adversarial network (GAN) with a quantum generator, classical discriminator, and a gradient penalty. It was trained selectively on inhibitors for BChE from the IPAD-DB database of Alzheimer's protein inhibitors. Post processing algorithms then checked for size, lipophilicity, planarity, aromatic rings, as well as other functional groups critical for determining effective molecules that target BChE. Out of complete generation of 100 molecules, 100% were small, 89% had amine groups, 30% passed BBB permeability checkers, and 93% were predicted to have low aggregation potential. From molecular docking, 97% were able to interact, with the binding affinity between the main structure of the generated inhibitors to BCHE ranging -2.8 to -4.4, indicating weak binding capability. This approach offers the framework to accelerate the discovery of promising therapeutics for Alzheimer's disease by shortening the amount of time needed in the lab to find a molecule with optimized properties for BBB penetration and BChE inhibition.

United States Association for Young Physicists Tournament (USAYPT)

Eddy Currents and Braking Forces

Description: When a permanent magnet moves relative to a nonmagnetic conductor, Eddy currents are induced and a force pair arises that tends to oppose the relative motion. Explore this phenomenon theoretically and experimentally, with particular attention to the magnitude of the force and its dependence on speed. Consider at least an azimuthally symmetric magnet moving through a cylindrical tube, but feel free to include other geometries and the effect of parameters other than speed.
